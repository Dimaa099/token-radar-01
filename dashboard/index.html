How every price on the board is made

One function prices every row. Nothing is priced by name, and no figure comes from outside the snapshot:

implied value = TVL × category multiple × growth × runway × breadth × size
  • Category multiple. For each DefiLlama category, take the tokenized protocols in it and their market-cap-to-TVL ratios; the multiple is the median ratio, shrunk toward the global median by the peer count (shrink 6) and clamped between 0.03 and 1.20. A category with fewer than three peers prices at the global median. The table below is the actual pricing table this board ran on.
  • Growth. 1 + clamp(7-day TVL change, -60%, +120%) × 0.5: a protocol adding TVL fast is worth more at launch than one bleeding it, and one week's move is capped so it cannot dominate the price.
  • Runway. 0.78 + 0.44 × the square root of its token-free age over four years: the longer a protocol has run with no token, the more usage a launch would capitalise. A protocol with no tracking date is priced at the snapshot's median age.
  • Breadth. +2.5% per chain past the first, up to eight: distribution is worth a premium, and it is the smallest factor on purpose.
  • Size. The multiple is tilted by (this TVL / a reference TVL) to the power -0.25. The comparables' own record demands it: across the snapshot's tokenized protocols, the median market-cap-to-TVL ratio falls from 0.48 for the smallest third to 0.05 for the largest — the market simply does not pay a giant the multiple it pays a small protocol, so this board does not either. The slope was fitted, not guessed: tested out of sample on the tokenized protocols (each priced blind, itself left out), a slope of 0.25 beat the untilted model on the held-out half of all 24 seeded splits, with the ranking correlation intact; steeper slopes kept buying a little error with a lot of ranking. The reference is the median TVL of the protocol's own category peers — a protocol is large or small next to the protocols it is actually priced against — falling back to the whole peer set's median when the category is thin. That yardstick was itself put to the same out-of-sample test before it shipped, against a second set of splits the selection never saw.
  • Thin evidence is marked, not hidden — and it is evidence, not a shrug. A category with fewer than three tokenized peers cannot carry its own median. An earlier version of this model priced such rows at the bare global median, which left the board's largest rows — the thin-category giants — resting on no local evidence at all. Now a thin row is priced from the eight tokenized protocols nearest it in TVL, whatever they do: the market's price for a protocol's size is real evidence about a protocol of that size. Their median is blended half-and-half with the global median in log space, the row is marked thin, and its detail view names the neighbours' median it used. Read a thin row as a skip-grade price — a reason to look closer, not a number to size a farm by.
  • Every plan is netted to a verdict. A plan's capital is not a cost — it sits in the position and returns on unwind — so each plan is netted to what it actually spends, its gas, and set against the launch the board prices: the detail view states the break-even share, the slice of the launch at which the allocation's worth covers the gas, and a verdict struck at a reference allocation of 0.01% of the launch (Farm at ten times covered, Farm small at twice, Watch below that). The tail rows whose implied value is too small for a small wallet's plausible share to pay their gas are told so, in numbers, on their own row.
  • The pricing table is rounded before it prices. Every figure this page prints — the category medians, the global median, the reference TVLs — is the exact figure the price was made from, so the detail view can recompute any row's price from the printed figures and land on the board's number to the dollar. It does, in this page's own code, every time a row is opened.
  • Who gets ranked. Every watchlist protocol, plus every other protocol that records no token. Above the watchlist floor a candidate must also pass the watchlist's own product screen, so a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that already trades (the snapshot's largest token-free records are exactly those: the wrapped bitcoins and exchange staking wrappers) is never ranked as a launch still to come. Below the floor the net is cast widest, because finding the early ones is the radar's job.

The model, graded blind on every run

A pricing model for protocols whose answer is not known yet can still be marked — on the protocols whose answer is. Every time this program runs, it takes each tokenized protocol in the snapshot, hides its market cap, rebuilds its category's multiple without it, and prices it with the same function that prices this board. Then it compares the blind price with the market cap the protocol actually carries. No protocol ever prices itself, and the figures below are recomputed from the sealed snapshot on the run that wrote this page — they are not a claim pasted in from somewhere else.

What the board does not do: predict that a token will be issued, or when. A protocol can run for years with no token and never issue one. The farm plans assume the reader wants the position anyway if the protocol's own rewards make it worthwhile, and every plan says never to pay anyone to "claim" an airdrop.

Every pre-token protocol, accounted for

A coverage claim you cannot check is a slogan, so here is the whole field: every protocol in the snapshot that records no token, ranked on the board or set aside below with the reason the board's own screens produced — an exchange's own product and a versioned pool are out at any size, and above the watchlist floor the watchlist's own product screen applies, so a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that already trades never poses as a launch still to come. Nothing pre-token is dropped silently.

Launch Radar: a program reads one sealed snapshot and writes this page; no network, no tracking.
